Deathknell — Tirisfal Glades

A Different Kind of Enemy

The Scourge had been simple.

Alisander understood that now.

They attacked.

He defended himself.

They obeyed commands that were not their own.

He destroyed them.

There had been unpleasant implications in the work, certainly, but very little ambiguity.

Deathknell rarely permitted such luxury for long.

When Alisander returned to the ruined settlement, another Forsaken was waiting within the old structure occupied by Shadow Priest Sarvis.

A woman dressed in red.

Green hair framed a face already marked by death.

Novice Elreth.

She regarded Alisander with the peculiar calm he had begun to recognize among Forsaken who had survived their own awakening long enough for catastrophe to become routine.

Her problem was not a ghoul.

Nor a skeleton.

Nor another corpse requiring transport.

It was a woman.

A frightened one.

Alisander already suspected who she meant.

Lilian Voss.

He had seen her before.

He remembered the terror in her voice.

The absolute certainty with which she had rejected the evidence of her own condition.

She had not merely disliked being undead.

She had refused to believe it.

Elreth had seen her running toward the old inn.

Screaming.

Pleading to be taken home.

Alisander listened without interruption.

He remembered the conclusion he had reached only a short time ago.

The Forsaken were corpses who could say no.

Apparently Deathknell was about to test how seriously he believed it.

The Hand Mirror

Elreth held out a small mirror.

Alisander looked at it.

Then at her.

The object seemed absurdly delicate in Deathknell.

Glass.

A frame.

A polished surface intended for people who still cared whether their hair was properly arranged.

Among the Forsaken, mirrors performed a different service.

They testified.

Elreth wanted him to find Lilian and show her the reflection.

Not explain it.

Not persuade her through argument.

Simply make denial impossible.

Lilian needed to see what she really was.

Elreth believed that once she accepted the truth, perhaps she would join the Forsaken cause.

Then came the final justification.

Deathknell could use every body it could get.

Alisander stared at the mirror again.

Bodies.

Saltain had spoken the same language.

Corpses as potential.

Numbers as survival.

Necessary arithmetic.

He understood it.

That did not mean he liked hearing it applied to Lilian.

There was a dangerous distance between accepting what you were and becoming what someone else needed you to be.

Alisander took the mirror.

Its glass caught the violet light.

For a moment he saw part of his own face reflected there.

Gray skin.

Red eyes.

The edge of his orange mohawk.

He turned the mirror away.

One crisis at a time.

The Old Inn

The inn stood inside Deathknell like the remains of a memory nobody had bothered to bury.

Once, perhaps, it had held warmth.

Conversation.

Food.

Travellers complaining about weather and prices and beds.

Now broken windows admitted the dead light of Tirisfal.

Timber groaned in the wind.

Dust occupied the rooms more faithfully than any guest.

Alisander entered.

His steps sounded too loud.

He did not call Lilian's name immediately.

Fear had driven her here.

Announcing himself as another corpse from Deathknell seemed unlikely to improve matters.

He climbed farther inside.

Then he heard movement.

Quick.

Nervous.

Someone trying very hard not to be found.

Alisander stopped.

“Lilian.”

Silence.

He waited.

“I am not here to hurt you.”

That sentence sounded considerably less reassuring when spoken by an undead man inside a ruined inn.

Alisander made a note to improve his technique.

◆

Lilian Voss

She emerged slowly.

Alisander remembered her immediately.

The same young woman.

The same desperate conviction that some terrible misunderstanding had occurred.

She looked at him as though his existence itself were an accusation.

Perhaps it was.

Alisander did not move closer.

“Elreth asked me to find you.”

The name did not calm her.

Nothing in Deathknell did.

Lilian wanted to go home.

She wanted the world to return to whatever shape it had possessed before she awakened beneath the dead sky.

Alisander understood the impulse.

More than he wanted to admit.

The difference between them was not acceptance.

Not really.

Alisander had simply reached the questions sooner.

Lilian was still fighting the premise.

He wondered whether that made her weaker.

He decided it did not.

Denial could be foolish.

It could also be the final barricade a mind erected before an unbearable truth.

Breaking that barricade carelessly could destroy more than ignorance.

Alisander reached slowly into his robe.

Lilian recoiled.

He stopped.

Then withdrew the mirror.

Nothing more.

“You need to see something.”

◆

The Reflection

Lilian stared at the mirror.

At first she did not understand.

Then she saw herself.

Alisander watched the change happen.

Confusion first.

Then recognition.

Then horror.

The reflected face belonged to no living woman.

Death had written itself across her features whether she accepted the handwriting or not.

Lilian touched her face.

The reflection did the same.

There was nowhere left for denial to retreat.

Alisander said nothing.

Nothing he could say would improve the truth.

The mirror had already said too much.

Lilian's expression shattered.

Whatever fragile arrangement had allowed her to continue believing that she could simply return home collapsed at once.

She recoiled from the glass.

From Alisander.

From herself.

Her fear became anger.

Her anger became panic.

Then she ran.

Truth had won.
Alisander was not convinced that meant anyone else had.
◆

Truth Without Mercy

Alisander remained inside the inn.

Lilian's footsteps disappeared into Deathknell.

The silence that followed felt heavier than the one left behind by dead Scourge.

He looked down at the mirror.

Elreth had been correct.

Lilian needed to understand what had happened to her.

A person could not make an informed choice while denying the reality in which the choice existed.

But correctness was an inadequate measure of what had just happened.

Alisander had learned that from magic long ago.

A spell could be technically perfect and still be catastrophic.

Truth was apparently capable of the same thing.

He thought again of the Scourge.

Creatures without choice.

Then of Lilian.

A woman with choice who desperately wanted reality to offer her a different set of options.

Free will did not guarantee good choices.

Nor comfortable ones.

It did not guarantee acceptance.

It merely guaranteed that the answer belonged to the person giving it.

◆

The Right to Refuse

Elreth had wanted Lilian to join their cause.

Alisander understood why.

Deathknell needed defenders.

Workers.

Soldiers.

Mages.

Priests.

Bodies capable of becoming citizens.

The living world surrounding Tirisfal did not look kindly upon the Forsaken.

Survival demanded numbers.

But necessity did not erase the principle Alisander had spent his new existence discovering.

Valdred had accepted.

Redpath had refused.

Lilian had fled.

None of those answers made them less capable of choice.

Quite the opposite.

If Deathknell only respected free will when a newly risen corpse chose Deathknell, then the principle was meaningless.

Choice mattered most when the answer was inconvenient.

Perhaps especially when it was painful.

A freedom that required the correct answer was only obedience wearing better clothes.

Alisander wondered whether Elreth would agree.

He suspected she might.

He also suspected Deathknell would continue testing the distinction.

◆

The Mirror Looks Back

Before returning the mirror, Alisander stopped beneath one of the broken windows.

Pale light fell across the glass.

He looked into it.

Deliberately this time.

The face staring back remained unpleasant.

Gray flesh.

Glowing red eyes.

A ridiculous orange crest of hair that had somehow survived whatever process had failed to preserve the rest of him properly.

Alisander tilted his head.

The corpse in the mirror did the same.

He searched the face for recognition.

Nothing.

No memory surfaced.

No forgotten name.

No sudden revelation that the reflection belonged to the man he remembered being.

But neither did he recoil.

That difference mattered.

Lilian had looked into the mirror and seen proof that everything she believed about herself had been destroyed.

Alisander looked into it and saw a question.

Questions he could work with.

He raised one hand.

Blue-white arcane light formed between his fingers.

The mirror caught that too.

Dead face.

Living thought.

Arcane power.

An impossible combination.

Yet there it was.

Alisander lowered the mirror.

Perhaps accepting the truth did not mean liking it.

Perhaps it did not even mean understanding it.

It meant beginning from what was real.

Lilian was not ready to do that.

Alisander could not blame her.

But he had made a different choice.

He would look directly at what death had made of him.

He would question it.

Study it.

Test every assumption surrounding it.

And when the time came to decide what the reflection meant—

the decision would be his.

The grave could change his face.
It did not get to decide the man looking back.

The chronicle continues. Lilian Voss had been forced to confront the truth of the grave. Alisander was beginning to understand that truth and acceptance were not the same thing—and that neither could replace choice.
