Deathknell — Tirisfal Glades

The Price of Tomorrow

Alisander had barely finished deciding what he intended to become when Deathknell presented him with another opportunity to test the answer.

Another voice called from beneath the diseased trees.

Another task.

Another problem.

He had begun to suspect that undeath possessed a sense of humor.

Deathguard Saltain waited near one of the ruined buildings at the edge of the settlement.

Darnell stood nearby.

That alone told Alisander that whatever was coming would probably involve lifting something unpleasant.

Saltain wasted little time.

Deathknell required bodies.

More specifically, it required corpses suitable for resurrection.

The Forsaken had been transporting their dead from Silverpine, Hillsbrad and wherever else bodies could be obtained.

But transporting corpses over long distances was inefficient.

Particularly when several perfectly serviceable ones were lying practically outside Deathknell's gates.

Alisander stared at Saltain.

He waited for some indication that the deathguard understood how extraordinary that sentence was.

None came.

Deathknell had an astonishing ability to make blasphemy sound like inventory management.

Saltain continued.

The last remnants of the Scarlet Crusade still roamed parts of Tirisfal.

Several of their dead littered the ruined buildings to the north and east.

Six corpses were required.

Alisander was to locate them.

Darnell would do the carrying.

Saltain looked toward the hulking Forsaken beside him.

Apparently Darnell looked strong enough to carry a few corpses.

Darnell offered no objection.

Alisander looked at him.

Darnell looked back.

Neither spoke.

There were some conversations friendship did not require.

Recruitment

The word bothered Alisander.

Recruitment.

Among the living, recruitment implied persuasion.

Promises.

Coin.

Duty.

Patriotism.

Occasionally desperation.

The Forsaken apparently began the process by collecting the candidate's corpse.

Alisander was not certain whether the distinction was amusing or horrifying.

Probably both.

He remembered the resurrection grounds.

Redpath.

Lilian.

Valdred.

Three minds returned to three bodies.

Three different answers to the same impossible question.

Caice had insisted that the choice afterward belonged to them.

Accept.

Reject.

Walk away.

But the body itself apparently received no vote regarding whether it would be brought to Deathknell in the first place.

Alisander found the distinction fascinating.

The Forsaken respected free will remarkably well for people who began the conversation by stealing your corpse.

He suspected there was a philosophy somewhere beneath the contradiction.

Or perhaps only necessity.

The two were frequently confused.

Darnell started walking.

Alisander followed.

The Scarlet Dead

The first corpse lay beside the remains of a stone wall.

It was difficult to imagine the body standing again.

Mud stained the clothing.

One arm rested beneath the torso at an unnatural angle.

The face had settled into the vacant expression common to all corpses, regardless of what convictions had occupied them in life.

Scarlet fabric remained visible beneath the dirt.

Alisander stopped several steps away.

Darnell waited.

“That one,” Alisander said.

Darnell approached the corpse.

There was no ceremony.

No hesitation.

He bent, took hold of the body and lifted it with the ease of a laborer collecting timber.

Alisander watched.

Yesterday, perhaps, the corpse had possessed a name.

Opinions.

Enemies.

Someone might have hated him.

Someone might have loved him.

Perhaps he had spent his final moments terrified.

Perhaps convinced.

Perhaps both.

Death had simplified him.

Now he was weight.

Material.

Potential.

A future citizen who did not yet know he had been nominated.

They continued.

Another body lay near a tree.

Another beside collapsed masonry.

A third had fallen so close to Deathknell that Alisander could see the violet glow of the settlement through the branches.

One by one he pointed them out.

One by one Darnell collected them.

The work became repetitive.

That was what made it disturbing.

Horror was supposed to announce itself.

Screams.

Blood.

Monsters.

This was quieter.

Find the body.

Mark the body.

Carry the body.

Continue.

Bureaucracy again.

Alisander was beginning to suspect it might be the most powerful necromantic force in Deathknell.

◆

Darnell's Burden

Darnell carried the dead without complaint.

Alisander found himself watching him more than the corpses.

There was nothing careless in Darnell's movements.

Nothing reverent either.

He simply did what needed to be done.

Alisander had envied that quality before.

He envied it again.

Darnell did not appear interested in solving undeath.

He did not interrogate resurrection as though it were an equation waiting for the correct variables.

A task existed.

He performed it.

Another followed.

He continued.

There was strength in that simplicity.

Perhaps also danger.

“Does this bother you?” Alisander asked.

Darnell adjusted the corpse across his shoulder.

He considered the question.

“They're dead.”

Alisander looked at him.

“So are we.”

Darnell glanced back.

“Exactly.”

Then he kept walking.

Alisander stood motionless for several seconds.

He disliked how effective the answer was.

◆

Six Bodies

By the fourth corpse, Alisander had stopped imagining names.

By the fifth, he realized that he had stopped.

That disturbed him.

The same thing had happened with the Mindless Zombies.

Repetition converted horror into procedure.

The first death demanded reflection.

The sixth required efficiency.

He wondered whether that was how Deathknell survived.

Perhaps no society of the dead could function if every corpse remained a tragedy.

Eventually somebody had to count them.

Somebody had to move them.

Somebody had to decide which could be raised.

Somebody had to decide what happened when the result went wrong.

Mordo repaired bodies.

Caice repaired minds where he could.

Saltain acquired replacements.

Alisander suddenly understood Deathknell differently.

It was not merely a graveyard.

It was infrastructure.

A machine built around one impossible fact:

death was no longer necessarily final.

And machines required material.

The sixth Scarlet corpse lay beneath a dead tree.

Alisander pointed.

Darnell approached.

Then Alisander raised a hand.

“Wait.”

Darnell stopped.

Alisander crouched beside the corpse.

Nothing distinguished this one from the others.

That was precisely the problem.

He wondered what the man would say if the Val'kyr restored his mind.

Would he scream?

Attack?

Run?

Accept?

Would he become another Valdred?

Another Lilian?

Another Redpath?

Or something entirely different?

A corpse was simple.
Resurrection was where the complications began.

Alisander stood.

“Take him.”

Darnell did.

◆

The Question of Consent

They began the return journey.

Alisander walked in silence.

Chapter by chapter, Deathknell seemed determined to give him increasingly inconvenient definitions of freedom.

Agatha had told the newly risen they were not slaves.

Caice had allowed Redpath to refuse.

Lilian had been permitted to run.

Valdred had chosen to remain.

Free will mattered.

Apparently deeply.

Yet before any of those choices could be made, someone else had chosen to raise them.

Alisander considered the contradiction.

Perhaps resurrection and allegiance were separate questions.

The Forsaken could return a mind to the world.

They could not command what that mind did afterward.

At least, that was the principle.

He found himself respecting it despite everything.

There was something strangely honest about a society that admitted it could give you existence but not meaning.

Meaning remained your responsibility.

Alisander understood that burden now.

He carried it more easily than he had yesterday.

Darnell carried corpses.

Everyone contributed according to talent.

◆

What the Forsaken Build

Deathguard Saltain received them with satisfaction.

Six bodies.

Mission complete.

The arithmetic was perfect.

Alisander suspected the future would not be.

Somewhere beyond his sight, the corpses would be prepared.

Perhaps raised.

Perhaps repaired first.

Perhaps one would awaken calm.

Another screaming.

Another mindless.

Another furious.

Six corpses did not mean six Forsaken.

It meant six possibilities.

That distinction mattered.

Saltain saw manpower.

Mordo saw bodies.

Caice saw damaged minds.

Alisander increasingly saw questions.

Perhaps that was his role.

Not to solve everything.

Not yet.

Merely to refuse easy explanations.

The Forsaken were rebuilding something here.

Not life.

Life was gone.

They were constructing a civilization from what remained afterward.

Corpses.

Memories.

Anger.

Will.

Necessity.

The materials were terrible.

Alisander had seen worse foundations.

◆

The Weight We Carry

Darnell disappeared toward his next duty.

Saltain turned his attention elsewhere.

Deathknell continued.

Alisander remained beneath the trees for a moment longer.

He looked at his hands.

He had done that often since awakening.

At first because they frightened him.

Then because they reminded him that he was different from the Mindless.

Now they meant something else.

Choice required a body capable of acting.

Will required a world in which to express itself.

Perhaps that was what Deathknell actually offered.

Not salvation.

Not redemption.

Opportunity.

Ugly.

Unrequested.

Imperfect.

But real.

Six dead Scarlets would soon be offered that same opportunity.

What they did with it would belong to them.

Alisander found himself hoping that was true.

Because if the Forsaken were to claim that free will separated them from the monsters they had once served—

then free will could not merely be a comforting phrase.

It had to survive inconvenience.

Rejection.

Anger.

Even rebellion.

Especially rebellion.

The thought lingered.

Alisander suspected Deathknell would eventually force him to test it.

For now, another task waited.

Another voice among the dead.

Another piece of this strange civilization revealing itself.

Blue-white arcane light flickered briefly between his fingers.

Alisander closed his hand.

Death had made his body useful to the Forsaken.
What he did with his mind remained his decision.

For the moment, that was enough.

The chronicle continues. Alisander had begun to understand how Deathknell survived: not by defeating death, but by turning what death left behind into possibility.
