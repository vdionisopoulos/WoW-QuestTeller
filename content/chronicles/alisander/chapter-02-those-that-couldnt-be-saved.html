Deathknell — Tirisfal Glades

The Dead Did Not All Awaken the Same

The dead did not all awaken the same.

Alisander learned that quickly.

Only a short time had passed since he had opened his eyes beneath the poisoned sky of Tirisfal, yet already the novelty of his own resurrection was beginning to fade. The impossible had become practical. The grave had released him, Undertaker Mordo had put him to work, and the world had continued as though death were merely another inconvenience to be managed.

But not every corpse rose with a mind capable of understanding what had happened.

Some woke screaming. Some ran. Some stared into the darkness and saw nothing at all.

And some became monsters.

A Choice Denied

Undertaker Mordo stood among the graves with the same calm expression he seemed to reserve for everything concerning death. Around him, more bodies were being pulled back into the world.

Alisander watched one of them rise.

Blue-white energy surrounded the newly awakened corpse as unseen forces dragged consciousness back into flesh that had already begun to decay.

A voice called out to the dead.

“Rise from death’s slumber and join your brothers!”

The words should have sounded triumphant.

They did not.

The corpse recoiled.

“I never asked for this! Leave me alone!”

Alisander said nothing.

He understood the reaction better than he wanted to admit.

No one had asked him either.

The Mindless

Mordo explained what happened to those who could not endure the transition.

Some retained enough of themselves to function. Some fled into the woods, terrified and confused, unable to accept their new existence.

Others lost whatever fragile thread bound memory, identity and reason together.

They wandered without purpose.

They attacked anything that came near.

The Forsaken called them Mindless Zombies.

Alisander looked toward the nearby hillside. Several figures shuffled among the graves. Their movements were erratic. Their bodies were animated.

Their minds were gone.

Mordo's instructions were simple.

Destroy them.

Alisander stared at him.

“For their sake?”
“For ours.”

There was no ceremony. No mourning. No priest offered prayers for what remained of them.

The Forsaken had little patience for such things. The dead who could think had work to do. The dead who could not were a danger.

Alisander understood the logic.

That did not make it easier.

He looked again toward the mindless creatures roaming between the trees.

Former brothers and sisters, Mordo had called them.

Former.

The word bothered him.

How quickly did one cease to be a person? Was it when the heart stopped? When the body decayed? When memory disappeared?

Or only when others decided there was nothing left worth saving?

Alisander had no answer.

Not yet.

◆

Necessary Work

The first zombie noticed him before he reached the edge of the graveyard.

Its head snapped toward him.

For an instant, Alisander imagined recognition in its dead eyes.

Then it charged.

There was no hesitation in its movements. No fear. No confusion.

Only hunger and violence.

Alisander raised his hand.

Arcane energy gathered around his fingers.

The spell came more easily this time.

That disturbed him almost as much as the creature rushing toward him.

Magic responded before emotion could interfere.

Structure. Focus. Release.

Energy tore through the air.

The zombie collapsed.

Silence returned.

Alisander stood over it for several seconds.

Nothing moved.

Whatever had animated the corpse was gone.

But had there been anything inside it when he destroyed it?

That question lingered.

The second one came from behind a broken gravestone. The third wandered near the trees. The fourth attacked without warning.

By the fifth, Alisander no longer flinched.

By the sixth, he hated himself for noticing that.

Each spell became cleaner. More efficient. His movements sharpened.

The mage in him adapted quickly.

Too quickly.

There was a terrible comfort in precision.

A spell had no uncertainty. Either the structure was correct or it was not. Either the target stood or it fell.

Magic offered rules.
Death offered none.
◆

The Difference

When the last Mindless Zombie collapsed into the grass, Alisander lowered his hand.

The graveyard was quiet again.

He should have felt satisfaction. The task was complete. The danger had been removed.

Instead he found himself staring at the corpse.

It wore the remains of clothing that might once have belonged to a farmer. Or a merchant. Or a soldier.

It did not matter anymore.

No name remained. No history. No memory.

Only a body that had risen incorrectly.

Alisander looked down at his own hands.

Gray skin. Cold fingers. Arcane residue fading between them.

What separated him from the thing on the ground?

Luck? Will? Magic? Something in the ritual?

He remembered Mordo's words.

Some maintain their free will.

Others do not.

Alisander disliked explanations that depended upon chance.

Chance was merely ignorance wearing a respectable name.

There was a reason.

There was always a reason.

He intended to find it.

◆

Death Interrupted

On his way back to Mordo, another newly awakened Forsaken stumbled away from the resurrection grounds.

The figure shook violently.

“I never asked for this!”

Alisander stopped.

Neither had he.

For the first time, he allowed himself to consider the possibility that returning from death might not have been a gift.

Perhaps it had been theft.

A life taken.

A death interrupted.

A choice denied.

The living feared dying.

The Forsaken had discovered another fear entirely.

What if death was the one thing that belonged to you—
and even that could be taken away?
◆

Six

Mordo listened as Alisander reported the task complete.

Six Mindless Zombies destroyed.

No complications.

Efficient. Necessary.

The words sounded clinical.

Alisander preferred them that way.

He had no language yet for what he had actually done.

Mordo seemed satisfied.

Alisander was not.

He looked across Deathknell.

More corpses were awakening.

Some rose calmly. Some screamed. Some fled. Some would eventually lose themselves.

And somewhere among them, perhaps, was another Alisander—a corpse waking with enough memory to realize that the world had changed forever.

He wondered how many would survive their second birth.

He wondered how many would wish they had not.

◆

The Fear Beyond Death

That night, if night could truly be distinguished from day beneath the dead sky of Tirisfal, Alisander remained awake.

Not because he needed to.

Sleep had become an uncertain habit.

Instead, he watched pale arcane light gather in his palm.

The spell was perfect.

Predictable.

Obedient.

His own resurrection was none of those things.

He closed his fingers around the light until it vanished.

Six corpses had lost themselves.

He had not.

That difference now mattered more than anything.

Because if reason could be lost once—perhaps it could be lost again.

Alisander looked toward the graves.

He had survived death.

Now he understood that survival was not the same as remaining himself.

And for the first time since awakening, he feared something more than the grave.

He feared becoming empty.

A body without memory.

Power without purpose.

A name without a mind behind it.

Alisander rose.

Whatever undeath had made of him, he would learn its rules.

He would understand why some returned whole and others returned broken.

And if the answer did not exist—

he would create one.

Because death had already taken his life.

He would not allow it to take his mind as well.
