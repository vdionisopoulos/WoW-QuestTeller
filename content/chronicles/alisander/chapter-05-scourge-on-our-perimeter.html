Deathknell — Tirisfal Glades

Another Voice Among the Dead

The voice waiting for Alisander belonged to Shadow Priest Sarvis.

Somehow, that seemed appropriate.

Deathknell had already introduced him to undertakers, caretakers and deathguards.

Apparently the dead required priests as well.

Though Alisander suspected their sermons differed considerably from those of the living.

Sarvis stood within one of Deathknell's ruined structures, wrapped in the practiced stillness of someone who had long ago stopped finding the surrounding decay remarkable.

He studied Alisander carefully.

Not his robes.

Not the condition of his corpse.

His posture.

His hands.

The way his eyes followed every movement.

A mage, apparently, was still recognizable after death.

Sarvis came quickly to the point.

If Alisander intended to become an asset to the Forsaken, he needed to know how to fight.

Alisander almost smiled.

He had spent the last several hours killing the mindless dead and assisting in the collection of corpses.

Deathknell evidently considered this introductory material.

The Forsaken had unusual standards for a beginner.

Sarvis spoke of enemies beyond the settlement.

Wretched ghouls.

Rattlecage skeletons.

Remnants of the fallen Scourge.

Eight were to be destroyed.

Not because they had gone mad.

Not because resurrection had failed.

Because they remained what the Forsaken had once been.

Servants.

◆

What the Forsaken Were

Alisander had heard the word before.

Scourge.

It existed inside his broken memories like a stain beneath old parchment.

He remembered streets.

Screaming.

Dead things moving where dead things should not move.

He remembered fear.

Then darkness.

But the Forsaken spoke of the Scourge differently.

Not as distant monsters.

As history.

Their history.

Alisander understood enough now to recognize the distinction.

The Forsaken were undead.

So were the creatures outside Deathknell.

Flesh alone did not separate them.

Neither did rot.

Nor magic.

The difference was somewhere less visible.

Will.

Thought.

Choice.

The very things he had spent his new existence examining.

The Scourge represented what undeath became when those things were removed.

Alisander found himself suddenly more interested in Sarvis's assignment.

This was no longer merely combat training.

It was a comparison.

One he intended to study closely.

Beyond Deathknell

Alisander left the settlement alone.

The violet lights of Deathknell faded behind him as the forest closed around the road.

Tirisfal did not become more welcoming beyond the graves.

The trees leaned together like conspirators.

Fog settled among their roots.

Ruined stone emerged from the earth at strange angles.

Somewhere in the distance, something screamed.

Alisander paused.

He waited.

Nothing followed.

“Charming,” he murmured.

Blue-white light gathered faintly around his fingertips.

He no longer needed to search for the sensation.

Magic answered quickly now.

That pleased him.

It also raised another uncomfortable question.

Was he recovering something that had belonged to the man he had been?

Or was the creature called Alisander simply learning faster than expected?

For the moment, the distinction could wait.

Something moved between the trees.

◆

The Scourge Remain

The first ghoul emerged on all fours.

What remained of its flesh stretched tightly across an emaciated frame.

Its movements were too fast.

Too eager.

Its eyes contained nothing Alisander recognized as thought.

It saw him.

It charged.

Alisander raised his hand.

Arcane structure formed almost instinctively.

Focus.

Intent.

Release.

Blue-white energy crossed the distance between them.

The ghoul collapsed into the dead grass.

Alisander remained still.

No hesitation had come.

That was different from the Mindless Zombies.

Those creatures had disturbed him because they had once been newly risen Forsaken whose minds had failed.

This thing felt different.

Older.

Emptier.

Purpose without choice.

Animation without self.

A weapon that happened to be made of a corpse.

Alisander stared down at it.

Undeath was not the horror.
Being dead and belonging to someone else was.
◆

Rattlecage

The skeleton came next.

Alisander heard it before he saw it.

Bone clicking against bone.

A dry rhythm moving between the trees.

The creature emerged carrying the remains of a weapon.

There was something almost absurd about it.

No muscles.

No organs.

Barely enough material remaining to qualify as a corpse.

Yet necromancy compelled it onward.

Alisander stepped aside as the weapon descended.

He extended one hand.

Arcane power struck the skeleton squarely.

Bones exploded across the forest floor.

A skull rolled several feet and came to rest against a root.

Alisander looked at it.

“Efficient.”

He wondered whether Sarvis would approve of the assessment.

Probably.

Deathknell rewarded practicality.

He was beginning to understand why.

◆

Eight of the Dead

The third fell faster than the first.

The fourth faster still.

By the fifth, Alisander was no longer thinking about individual spells.

His body remembered.

His mind calculated.

Distance.

Movement.

Timing.

Power.

He adjusted instinctively.

A step to the side.

A turn of the wrist.

A fraction less energy when the target was already damaged.

A fraction more when bone proved stubborn.

This was familiar.

Not the forest.

Not the Scourge.

The discipline.

The silent mathematics beneath violence.

That belonged to him.

Whatever else death had taken, it had not taken that.

The sixth ghoul died before reaching him.

The seventh skeleton shattered beneath a concentrated burst of arcane force.

The eighth stood farther up the path.

Alisander stopped.

The creature turned toward him.

No recognition.

No anger.

No fear.

Only command.

Whatever master had once given that command was irrelevant.

The order remained.

Attack.

Alisander raised both hands.

Arcane light illuminated the trees.

For one instant the dead forest became blue-white daylight.

Then the spell struck.

Silence returned.

◆

The Difference

Alisander stood among the remains.

Bones.

Rotting flesh.

Broken weapons.

Eight creatures destroyed.

Sarvis would call the task complete.

Alisander found the arithmetic less interesting than before.

He thought instead about Saltain's corpses.

Six dead Scarlets waiting for the possibility of resurrection.

If they awakened with minds of their own, they would be offered choices.

These creatures had none.

That was the difference.

Not flesh.

Not magic.

Not whether the heart beat.

The Forsaken were corpses who could say no.

The Scourge were corpses who could not.

Alisander considered the simplicity of it.

He did not trust simple answers.

But this one had survived several examinations.

Perhaps free will truly was the foundation beneath everything Deathknell claimed to represent.

If so, then the principle was more fragile than its citizens seemed to understand.

Freedom could not exist only when the answer was convenient.

He had thought that before.

He believed it more strongly now.

Somewhere in the back of his mind, Marshal Redpath's refusal returned to him.

No.

One word.

But perhaps the most important word a Forsaken could possess.

◆

Sarvis's Assessment

Shadow Priest Sarvis was waiting when Alisander returned.

Alisander reported the number.

Eight.

Ghouls and skeletons destroyed.

No complications.

Sarvis seemed satisfied.

Alisander suspected the priest had expected nothing more.

Combat ability demonstrated.

Threat reduced.

Another useful Forsaken confirmed.

The logic was familiar now.

Saltain measured bodies.

Mordo measured structural integrity.

Caice measured sanity.

Sarvis measured usefulness.

Deathknell was constantly evaluating what its dead could become.

Alisander wondered what measurement he would use for himself.

Power was insufficient.

Survival was insufficient.

Obedience was certainly insufficient.

Understanding came closer.

But even that was only a tool.

Perhaps the true measure was choice.

What a person did once every command, tradition and expectation had been stripped away.

Death had stripped away a great deal from Alisander.

Eventually he would discover what remained underneath.

◆

Not All Undead Are Forsaken

Alisander left Sarvis and stepped back beneath the dead trees.

His fingers still remembered the spells.

Blue-white energy flickered once across his knuckles.

He allowed it to remain.

The Scourge outside Deathknell had possessed power.

They had possessed durability.

They had even possessed purpose.

None of those things had made them people.

That realization settled somewhere deep inside him.

He had spent his first hours of undeath asking why he had awakened with his mind intact.

Perhaps the more important question was what he intended to do with it.

Death could animate flesh.

Necromancy could command bone.

Magic could create movement.

None of those things created a self.

Whatever Alisander was now, he remained capable of choosing.

He could question.

Refuse.

Learn.

Change.

That was more than the Scourge possessed.

Perhaps it was more than many of the living understood they possessed.

Alisander looked toward the road.

Deathknell had begun as a grave.

Then it had become a laboratory.

Then a settlement.

Now it was becoming something else.

A boundary.

Behind him lived the dead who had reclaimed themselves.

Beyond it wandered the dead who had not.

Alisander closed his hand around the arcane light.

He was undead.
He was not Scourge.
The difference was his to defend.

For the first time, the word Forsaken felt less like a description of what had happened to him.

It felt like something he might choose to become.
