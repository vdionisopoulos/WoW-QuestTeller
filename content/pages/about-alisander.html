Background

What Alisander Remembers

Alisander's known existence begins twice.

The first beginning lies somewhere beyond the damaged boundary of his memory.

The second began beneath the dead trees of Deathknell.

He remembers fragments of the man who existed before undeath: books, candlelight, ink staining his fingers and the exacting architecture of spellcraft.

He remembers that magic demanded precision.

A misplaced word could ruin a spell. A failure of concentration could transform controlled power into catastrophe.

Arcane energy itself remains one of his strongest surviving memories.

Beautiful.

Precise.

Hungry.

His death is far less cooperative.

Somewhere behind the darkness are screaming voices, pain and glimpses of the dead moving where the dead should not move.

Then nothing.

No name from his former life has returned to him.

No family.

No home.

No explanation for how he died.

But one part of the man survived without hesitation.

The mage.

When Alisander first reached into the darkness for power, the Arcane answered him as though death had been merely an interruption.

Magic remembered him before he remembered himself.

Memory Fragments

  • Books and long periods of study.
  • Candlelight and ink-stained fingers.
  • Precise knowledge of Arcane spellcraft.
  • Screaming somewhere near the end of his former life.
  • Images of the dead walking.
  • Pain followed by absolute darkness.
  • No reliable memory yet of his former identity.
The Awakening

The Man Who Came Back

Deathknell immediately confronted Alisander with a question far more complicated than whether he was alive or dead.

What exactly had returned?

He soon discovered that resurrection did not guarantee identity.

Some newly raised Forsaken retained their minds.

Some awakened terrified and rejected what they had become.

Some fled.

Others returned without enough of themselves remaining to be called people at all.

The Mindless Zombies disturbed Alisander more deeply than his own corpse ever had.

They proved that a body could continue after the person inside it had disappeared.

From that moment onward, his greatest fear changed.

He no longer feared death most.

He feared emptiness.

He had survived death.
Now he intended to survive himself.
Philosophy

The Question of Choice

Deathknell has forced Alisander to confront the strange morality of Forsaken existence almost immediately.

Newly raised undead are told that they are not slaves.

They may remain.

They may refuse.

They may walk away.

Valdred Moray accepted his new existence.

Marshal Redpath rejected it.

Lilian Voss could barely accept that she had become one of the creatures she despised.

Their reactions showed Alisander something important.

Resurrection could restore consciousness.

It could not dictate what that consciousness chose to become.

Even Deathknell's disturbing practice of recovering corpses for resurrection eventually returned him to the same question.

A body could be collected.

A mind could perhaps be restored.

But allegiance could not honestly be called free if refusal was forbidden.

The Forsaken were corpses who could say no.

Alisander increasingly suspects that this simple distinction may be the foundation beneath everything the Forsaken claim to be.

Identity

Undead, But Not Scourge

Shadow Priest Sarvis provided the next piece of Alisander's developing identity.

Beyond Deathknell still wandered remnants of the Scourge: Wretched Ghouls and Rattlecage Skeletons driven by necromantic purpose without meaningful choice.

Alisander destroyed them.

In doing so, he discovered that being undead was not itself what horrified him.

The Scourge moved.

They fought.

They possessed purpose.

What they did not possess was themselves.

Alisander did.

He could question.

Refuse.

Learn.

Change.

He was undead.
He was not Scourge.
The difference was his to defend.

For the first time since awakening, the word Forsaken has begun to feel less like something done to him and more like something he may consciously choose to become.

Personality

The Architect of Questions

Alisander approaches undeath as though someone has constructed an enormous magical system and neglected to provide the documentation.

He assumes there must be rules.

Causes.

Mechanisms.

Exceptions.

His instinct is therefore not simply to accept the impossible, but to interrogate it.

He is analytical, skeptical and increasingly observant of the contradictions around him.

Magic remains easier for him than emotion.

Arcane structure obeys rules.

People rarely do.

His humor remains dry, particularly when Deathknell manages to convert something horrifying into routine administration.

Beneath that control, however, lies a genuine fear of losing the identity that survived death.

Roleplay Profile

Character Traits

Traits

  • Analytical: searches for rules inside impossible events.
  • Arcane Discipline: spellcraft feels more familiar than his own history.
  • Skeptical: distrusts convenient explanations.
  • Dry Wit: resurrection did not entirely kill his sense of irony.
  • Observant: notices the contradictions others have learned to ignore.

Bonds & Drives

  • Understand the truth of his death and resurrection.
  • Preserve the mind and identity that returned from the grave.
  • Master the Arcane because it remains his strongest connection to the man he once was.
  • Determine what truly separates Forsaken free will from Scourge enslavement.
  • Decide for himself what being Forsaken will mean.

Flaws & Fears

  • Can turn emotion into analysis rather than confronting it.
  • May pursue explanations long after curiosity becomes dangerous.
  • Distrusts simple answers even when they may be correct.
  • Increasing competence risks making violence feel procedural.
  • Fears losing his mind and identity more than he fears death itself.
Unanswered Questions

The Mysteries Alisander Carries

  • Who was Alisander before his death?
  • Where did he live?
  • How did he die?
  • Why did his Arcane knowledge survive so clearly when other memories did not?
  • Why do some raised corpses retain themselves while others become Mindless?
  • What exactly separates resurrection from necromantic enslavement?
  • Can Forsaken free will survive rejection, conflict and rebellion?
  • What will Alisander choose once Deathknell no longer defines his world?
